Controller simulator

Runs the FAO-56 soil water balance one day at a time with generated weather — there is no sensor feed. The comparator is a fixed timer set the way a groundskeeper would set it (from the month's average ET₀, blind to today's weather), so a full-year run agrees with the annual model. It waters every day of the year (the annual model scales to the irrigation season), so compare percentages, not volumes.

How the numbers are produced

Methodology

The model follows FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56. The structure is standard; the coefficients are textbook values and the baseline behaviour is assumed — see the Assumptions tab.

1 · Crop water requirement

Reference evapotranspiration scaled by a landscape coefficient gives each zone's need before losses.

ETc = ET0 × Kc
ET₀Reference evapotranspiration (mm/day), monthly normals for the reference climate.
KcLandscape coefficient — turf 0.80, beds 0.90, shrubs 0.55, drought-tolerant 0.30. A plant picked from the library carries its own class factor.
Effective rainFAO/AGLW dependable rain: 0.6·P − 10 up to 70 mm/month, 0.8·P − 24 above.

2 · Applied water after losses

Plants receive only a fraction of what the system delivers.

Applied = max(0, ETc − Peff) × Area ÷ Ea
EaMethod efficiency × watering-time survival × slope loss. Hose 0.55, spray 0.75, drip 0.90.
Fixed timerIgnores rain except a manual halving when a month exceeds 150 mm, and over-applies by an assumed 15%.
SeasonAnnual need is scaled by season days ÷ 365.

3 · Soil water balance

The simulator runs this each day; irrigation triggers when depletion reaches the allowable limit.

Dtoday = Dyesterday + ETc − Peff − Irrigation
TAWTotal available water in the root zone (mm) = AWC × root depth.
RAWp × TAW. Irrigate when depletion ≥ RAW.
Cycle-and-soakSplit where application rate exceeds infiltration, and never over three hours per valve.

4 · Savings, attribution and payback

Savings are the modelled difference between current practice and the proposed system.

Payback = CapEx ÷ (ΔWater × Tariff − O&M)
ShapleyEach measure's saving averaged over all 120 adoption orders, so attribution is order-independent.
O&M4% of capex per year (assumption). “Simple payback” without it is also computed.
Range400 seeded Monte-Carlo draws over overwatering, current efficiency and Kc give the P10–P90 band.

Why rule-based, not machine learning

Every verdict traces to a published coefficient and a visible arithmetic step, so a reviewer can check any figure by hand. The bundled dataset's labels are themselves a formula of the inputs (see docs/DATASET.md), so a model trained on it would only relearn that formula — it would add opacity without adding information. Measured meter and sensor data from a pilot zone is what would justify ML.

What this tool is, and is not

It is a transparent water-budget model that shows where an irrigation system is likely losing water and what each fix is worth, with its uncertainty stated. It is not a measurement: no meter, probe or weather station feeds it, and the current-use figure is a model of assumed practice. The saving becomes a result only when a pilot zone is metered before and after.

Known simplifications: annual need is scaled by season length rather than modelled by month; the soil water balance ignores deep percolation and capillary rise; runoff from over-fast application is flagged but not subtracted; harvest runs on monthly steps; greywater reuse needs treatment and regulatory approval that are not costed here.
